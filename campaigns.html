01 فلسطين

Palestine

Advocacy for the liberation of Palestine

The occupation of Palestine did not begin on 7 October 2023, and it did not begin in 1948. It began on 2 November 1917, when Britain’s Foreign Secretary, Arthur Balfour, promised “a national home for the Jewish people” in a land where Palestinian Arabs made up around 90% of the population. Britain wrote that promise into its rule over Palestine and spent the next three decades enabling the land to be settled and colonised.

In 1948 came the Nakba: around 750,000 Palestinians were driven from their homes and more than 500 towns and villages were emptied or destroyed. In 1967 Israel occupied the West Bank, East Jerusalem and Gaza. The genocide in Gaza since October 2023 is the latest chapter of a colonial project more than a century old. AFJ was founded in response to that genocide, and the liberation of Palestine remains at the heart of everything we do.

A century of colonisation

  • 1917: the Balfour Declaration promises Palestine to a settler movement without the consent of its people.
  • 1948: the Nakba — around 750,000 Palestinians expelled and more than 500 towns and villages emptied or destroyed.
  • 1967: Israel occupies the West Bank, East Jerusalem and Gaza.
  • Since October 2023: more than 73,000 Palestinians killed in Gaza, according to Gaza’s health ministry.

We call forAn end to the occupation and the siege, accountability for war crimes, and an end to British complicity, including arms sales to Israel.

02 لبنان

Lebanon

Solidarity with South Lebanon and Dahieh

The people of South Lebanon and Beirut's southern suburbs, known as Dahieh, have lived under repeated Israeli bombardment. In March 2026 Israel launched a new offensive, striking Dahieh, the south and the Bekaa Valley and ordering the residents of dozens of villages to leave their homes.

Strikes, evacuation orders and Israeli troops on Lebanese soil have continued since. We stand with the families of the south and of Dahieh, with their right to live safely on their own land, and with their steadfastness.

Key facts

  • 2 March 2026: Israel announced a new offensive, with strikes on Dahieh, Tyre and Baalbek, and ordered residents of 50 villages to leave.
  • June 2026: displacement orders for 29 towns forced families in the south to move north of the Zahrani River.

We call forAn end to Israeli strikes and to the occupation of Lebanese land, and the right of every displaced family to return home.

03 أذربيجان

Azerbaijan · the right to education

Our sisters in Azerbaijan, kept out of school for wearing the hijab

The Azerbaijani regime, ruling over a population that is 97% Muslim — around 60% of them Shia — has imposed a brutal hijab ban on schoolgirls and teachers. Since 12 October 2026, girls who wear the hijab have been turned away at the school gates, denied the most basic right to an education.

We will keep protesting for our sisters in Azerbaijan until the ban is lifted and every one of them can return to school.

Key facts

  • 12 October 2026: pupils and teachers wearing the hijab were refused entry to schools across Azerbaijan.
  • 37 people were detained in Baku that day, according to Azerbaijan's Interior Ministry; activists report more than 200 detentions nationwide (Amnesty International).

We call forLift the ban, so that every girl and every teacher can return to school wearing her hijab — and free everyone detained for protesting it.

04 البحرين

Bahrain · persecution of the Shia

Injustices against the Shia of Bahrain

Bahrain's Shia majority has faced more than a decade of systematic repression since the 2011 pro-democracy uprising. Opposition leaders are still serving life sentences, the main Shia opposition society, al-Wefaq, was dissolved, and the senior scholar Sheikh Isa Qassim was stripped of his citizenship.

In 2026 the crackdown deepened. Rights groups report that more than 600 people have been arrested or summoned since February, that over fifty Shia clerics, preachers and reciters have been arrested or prosecuted, and that Ashura was restricted — black clothing banned, banners taken down and mourners, including children, summoned or arrested.

Key facts

  • Prominent opposition leaders have been jailed for more than a decade over the 2011 protests, four of them for life.
  • Ashura 2026: processions restricted and religious symbols removed from public spaces, according to a joint statement by six human rights organisations (3 August 2026).

We call forFree Bahrain's political prisoners and end the persecution of Shia religious life.

05 پاکستان

Pakistan

The massacres of Shias in Pakistan

For decades, Shia Muslims across Pakistan have been hunted for their faith. Mosques and imambargahs have been bombed during Friday prayers, mourners attacked in Muharram, and whole communities targeted — from the Hazara of Quetta to the worshippers of Shikarpur in Sindh. Armed sectarian groups such as Lashkar-e-Jhangvi have openly claimed the killings, while the state has failed to protect those they target.

Nowhere is this clearer than in Kurram district in the north-west, where the mostly Shia city of Parachinar has endured decades of massacres — Pakistan's own Interior Ministry counts 1,405 people killed in twenty years. In November 2024 a convoy of Shia travellers was ambushed near Ochat and more than forty people were killed. The main road was then blockaded for months, cutting off food, fuel and medicine; doctors reported children dying because they could not be treated.

We advocate for every Shia in Pakistan: for their safety, for justice for the victims, and for their right to practise their faith without fear. We also stand with the people of Kashmir, under Indian occupation since the region's special status was revoked in 2019.

Key facts

  • January & February 2013: two bombings targeting the Hazara Shia of Quetta killed more than 100 and at least 91 people; Lashkar-e-Jhangvi claimed both.
  • 30 January 2015: a suicide bombing at the central imambargah in Shikarpur, Sindh, killed more than 50 worshippers at Friday prayers.
  • 21 November 2024: the Ochat convoy attack in Kurram, followed by a months-long blockade of Parachinar.

We call forProtection and justice for the Shia of Pakistan, an end to impunity for those who kill them, and an end to the occupation of Kashmir.

06 البقيع

Saudi Arabia · Medina & the holy places

Jannat al-Baqi and our right to worship in the holy places

Beside the Prophet’s Mosque in Medina lies Jannat al-Baqi, the oldest Islamic cemetery in the city and one of the most sacred places in Islam. Here rest the Prophet’s infant son Ibrahim, his wives, his aunts, Fatima bint Asad — the mother of Imam Ali — and thousands of his companions. Here, too, lie four of the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt, the Prophet’s own household.

Who are the Shia?

Shia Muslims are those who follow the Ahl al-Bayt. We believe that after the Prophet Muhammad, leadership of the Muslim community passed by divine appointment to Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib, and after him to eleven Imams from his descendants with Fatima al-Zahra, the Prophet’s daughter. Their example — above all Imam Hussain’s stand at Karbala — teaches that faith means standing for justice. Today Shia Muslims make up roughly a tenth of the world’s Muslims.

Four of those Imams are buried in al-Baqi: Imam Hasan al-Mujtaba, Imam Ali Zayn al-Abidin, Imam Muhammad al-Baqir and Imam Ja’far al-Sadiq. Many Shia also believe that Fatima al-Zahra herself rests in al-Baqi, in a grave whose place is unknown.

For centuries the graves stood beneath domes and shrines where pilgrims came to pray. In 1806 Wahhabi forces allied with the House of Saud tore them down; the Ottomans rebuilt them. Then, on 8 Shawwal 1344 AH (21 April 1926), the forces of Ibn Saud levelled the cemetery again — the shrines of the Imams, the Prophet’s family and his companions reduced to rubble and bare stones. Every year, on 8 Shawwal, Shia Muslims around the world mourn the day and call for the shrines to be rebuilt. The Saudi authorities have refused every request.

The injustice did not end in 1926. Women are barred from entering al-Baqi and can only look on from behind its fences. Access is tightly limited, and at al-Baqi and the Prophet’s own resting place, Shia pilgrims have long been stopped from reciting supplications, harassed by religious police and even arrested. Shia are prevented from praying in congregation, and Shia communities inside Saudi Arabia have been pressured to stop communal prayers and to close their prayer halls.

Key facts

  • 8 Shawwal 1344 AH (21 April 1926): the shrines of al-Baqi were demolished, after an earlier destruction in 1806.
  • Four Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt, the Prophet’s son Ibrahim, his wives and thousands of companions are buried in al-Baqi.
  • 2009: Saudi security forces cracked down on Shia pilgrims at al-Baqi; dozens were arrested and a 15-year-old was reportedly shot (Human Rights Watch).
  • 2023: men were allowed to recite prayers at al-Baqi during Hajj; women may still only pay respects from behind the fences.

We call forThe shrines of al-Baqi to be rebuilt, the cemetery opened to women and to every pilgrim, and Shia Muslims to be free to pray, in congregation and in their own way, in the holy places.
