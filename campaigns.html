Britain

Ending British complicity — the imperial core

I · NationalII · Students

INational

Our struggle begins where we live. It was Britain that signed away Palestine in the Balfour Declaration of 1917, and Britain remains complicit today. In September 2024 the government suspended just 30 of its 350 arms export licences to the Zionist entity — and deliberately exempted parts for the F-35 fighter jets bombing Gaza, around 15% of which are made in Britain. From RAF Akrotiri in Cyprus, British spy planes flew more than 500 surveillance flights over Gaza in the first fifteen months of the genocide.

At home, those who stand against the genocide are being criminalised. In July 2025 the government used terrorism law to ban the direct-action group Palestine Action, and since then more than 3,000 people have been arrested — many of them simply for holding a sign. In June 2026, four of the Filton 25, activists who damaged a Zionist weapons factory near Bristol, were sentenced to a combined 26 years with a ‘terrorist connection’ attached to a conviction for criminal damage.

Free our sister

Fatema Zainab Rajwani

Fatema Zainab Rajwani is a 21-year-old Shia Muslim woman — our sister — sentenced in June 2026 to five years and eight months, with a ‘terrorist connection’ attached to a conviction for criminal damage. Her ‘crime’: trying to stop the drones that kill Palestinians.

The factory at Filton belongs to Elbit Systems, the Zionist entity’s largest weapons maker. The activists disabled around 40 weapons systems inside it, including:

  • Kestrel — targeting software that tracks moving people in real time, so drones can find and strike them.
  • Magni X quadcopters — used to watch Gaza and gather intelligence for attacks.
  • Thor drones — which drop bombs on Gaza and anchor Legion X, the AI network that links drones into swarms that hunt together.
  • Lanius — Latin for ‘butcher’ — small drones built to fly through windows and doors to kill the people inside.
  • Easy Aerial drones — from a company founded and run by former ‘Israeli’ soldiers.

Campaigners link drones like these to some of the worst crimes of the genocide: the killing of Al Jazeera journalists Samer Abu Daqqa and Hamza Al Dahdouh, the strike on a Palestine Red Crescent ambulance, and quadcopters opening fire on crowds waiting for flour. Fatema Zainab did not kill anyone. She is in prison for trying to stop the machines that do.

Follow @freethefilton25

  • September 2024: 30 of 350 arms licences suspended — with F-35 components exempted.
  • Dec 2023 – Mar 2025: at least 518 RAF surveillance flights over Gaza from RAF Akrotiri.
  • July 2025: Palestine Action banned under terrorism law; more than 3,000 people arrested by April 2026.

IIStudents

Our universities are meant to be places of learning and conscience. Instead, students who speak for Palestine are suspended, expelled and smeared, while Zionist institutions are given a free hand on campus.

The Union of Jewish Students

UJS claims to speak for every Jewish student in Britain, yet its own constitution commits it to promoting engagement with ‘Israel’, and it belongs to the World Union of Jewish Students, which operates under the World Zionist Organization. In Al Jazeera’s 2017 investigation The Lobby, a former UJS presidential candidate was filmed saying that the ‘Israeli’ embassy gives UJS money, and the union was shown working to unseat Malia Bouattia, the first Black Muslim woman to lead the National Union of Students. In 2022 her successor, Shaima Dallali, was dismissed after complaints led by UJS; in 2024 the NUS settled her claim and accepted that anti-Zionist beliefs may be protected beliefs.

The landmark case

Professor David Miller v University of Bristol

Professor David Miller, a sociologist at Bristol, was sacked in October 2021 after a high-profile campaign against him led by the university’s UJS-affiliated Jewish Society, which organised complaints and a petition and framed his call for an “end of Zionism” as a threat to Jewish students. Miller said the campaign had “manufactured hysteria”, and that it was Arab and Muslim students who had been made unsafe.

In February 2024 an employment tribunal ruled that he had been unfairly dismissed and discriminated against because of his beliefs — and found, for the first time, that his belief that Zionism is “inherently racist, imperialist and colonial” is a protected philosophical belief under the Equality Act 2010. In August 2026 the Employment Appeal Tribunal rejected almost all of Bristol’s appeal.

“I am also very proud that we have managed to establish that anti-Zionist views qualify as a protected belief under the UK Equality Act.” — Professor David Miller

Zionism Off Campus

British universities employ and platform former soldiers of the ‘Israeli’ Occupation Forces (IOF) to teach the very students whose families that army is bombing. At City St George’s, University of London, economics professor Michael Ben-Gad has publicly called himself an ‘IDF veteran’ who plans to ‘act like one’, and described protesting students as ‘modern brown shirts’. He also claimed students threatened to behead him — a claim with no evidence to support it, which plays into dangerous Islamophobic tropes.

Since the David Miller ruling, anti-Zionism is a protected belief under British equality law, and Palestinian, Arab and Muslim students have every right to a campus where they are safe. We stand with the Zionism Off Campus campaign — and with students demanding that City St George’s divests from the 44 arms-trade links that tie it to genocide.

Defend Haya Adam

Haya Adam, president of the SOAS Palestine Society, organised at the Liberated Zone and spoke out relentlessly against her university’s complicity in genocide. After a year-long, open-ended suspension and repeated disciplinary action, SOAS expelled her in August 2025, midway through her degree — the first expulsion of its kind for Palestine activism in Britain, campaigners say. It is a warning meant to frighten every student into silence. We will not be silent.

02 فلسطين

Palestine

Advocacy for the liberation of Palestine

The occupation of Palestine did not begin on 7 October 2023, and it did not begin in 1948. It began on 2 November 1917, when Britain’s Foreign Secretary, Arthur Balfour, promised “a national home for the Jewish people” in Palestine. Britain wrote that promise into its rule over Palestine and spent the next three decades enabling the land to be settled and colonised.

Then came the Nakba — Arabic for ‘the catastrophe’. Between 1947 and 1949, the Zionists drove out around 750,000 Palestinians. More than 500 towns and villages were emptied, many of them demolished completely, and massacres such as Deir Yassin on 9 April 1948 spread terror ahead of the advancing forces. In December 1948 the UN set out the refugees’ rights, including the right of Palestinian refugees to return to their homes. The Zionist entity continues to leave Palestinians forcibly exiled from their indigenous homeland.

Today UNRWA registers more than 5.5 million Palestinian refugees, still waiting to return home. That is why Palestinians speak of an ongoing Nakba: the Naksa of 1967, when the rest of Palestine was seized, the expansion of settlements, the siege of Gaza and the genocide since October 2023 are all chapters of the same colonial project. AFJ was founded in response to that genocide, and the liberation of Palestine remains at the heart of everything we do.

There are over 9,400 Palestinian hostages held in Zionist regime dungeons — men, women and children — thousands of them without charge or trial. Stay up to date with the Red Ribbons Campaign, calling for their freedom.

A century of colonisation

  • 1917: the Balfour Declaration promises Palestine to a settler movement without the consent of its people.
  • 1947–49: the Nakba — around 750,000 Palestinians expelled and more than 500 towns and villages emptied or destroyed.
  • 11 December 1948: UN Resolution 194 sets out the refugees’ rights, including the right of Palestinian refugees to return to their homes. It has never been honoured.
  • 1967: the Naksa (‘the setback’) — in just six days, the Zionist entity illegally seized the rest of Palestine: the West Bank, East Jerusalem and the Gaza Strip, along with Syria’s Golan Heights and Egypt’s Sinai Peninsula.
  • Today: over 9,400 Palestinians held in Zionist prisons, more than 3,500 of them under ‘administrative detention’ — with no charge and no trial.
  • Since October 2023: more than 73,000 Palestinians killed in Gaza, according to Gaza’s health ministry — and the true toll is far higher. A study in The Lancet Global Health estimated 75,200 violent deaths in the first 15 months alone, about 50% more than officially recorded for that period.

We call forThe total liberation of Palestine, the dismantling of Zionism and the return of Palestinians to their homes.

03 لبنان

Lebanon

Solidarity with Lebanon

On 8 October 2023, the Lebanese resistance bravely opened a front in solidarity with Palestine. But the Zionist occupation of Lebanon, and the massacre of its people, did not begin then. The Zionist entity invaded Lebanon in 1978 and again in 1982, besieging Beirut, and occupied the south of the country for 22 years until the resistance drove it out on 25 May 2000.

We remember Sabra and Shatila. Over three days in September 1982, with the Zionist army surrounding the camps and lighting up the night with flares, its allied militia slaughtered Palestinian refugees and Lebanese residents — over 3,000 people. The UN General Assembly passed a resolution declaring the massacre an “act of genocide”. Despite this, no individuals were held accountable for the crimes committed.

We remember Khiam Prison, where for fifteen years Lebanese men and women were held without trial and tortured — beaten, electrocuted and locked in cages — by the IOF and ‘Israeli’ intelligence, while the Red Cross was kept out.

And it continues today. The Zionist entity goes on massacring Lebanese people every day. Since its new war on Lebanon began in March 2026 it has killed thousands and forced more than 1.2 million people from their homes, and more than 60 Lebanese villages remain under illegal occupation. Lebanese hostages, kidnapped from the south, are held in Zionist dungeons. Stay updated via @eye.on.llebanon.

Key facts

  • 16–18 September 1982: the Sabra and Shatila massacre in Beirut.
  • 1985–2000: Khiam Prison, where detainees were tortured under the Zionist occupation of the south.
  • Since March 2026: at least 2,869 people killed in Lebanon by 12 May, and more than 1.2 million displaced.
  • May 2026: Lebanon’s Prime Minister says the Zionist army controls 68 villages in the south.

We call forAn end to the massacres and to the occupation of Lebanese land, freedom for the Lebanese hostages, and the right of every displaced family to return home.

04 ایران

Iran · resisting empire

The Islamic Republic of Iran, targeted for standing against Western hegemony

For more than four decades, Iran has been sanctioned, besieged and threatened for one reason: it refused to bow. Since the 1979 revolution it has rejected Western domination of the region, refused to recognise the Zionist entity, and stood with Palestine and the resistance while governments around it normalised with the occupier. Its people have paid for their noble stance with crippling sanctions — and now with bombs.

On 28 February 2026, the United States and the Zionist entity launched a war on Iran. On the first day they assassinated Ayatollah Sayyid Ali Khamenei, and struck homes, schools and hospitals across the country. By the April ceasefire, more than 3,400 people had been killed in Iran, at least 254 of them children. In July the ceasefire collapsed and the attacks resumed.

And Britain is not neutral. On 1 March, Keir Starmer allowed the US to use British bases for what he called ‘defensive’ operations. RAF Fairford in Gloucestershire became the main base in Europe for the US bombers attacking Iran — around 15 B-1Bs, over half the US fleet, and six B-52s, loaded with 2,000lb bombs and long-range cruise missiles. A former RAF squadron leader told Declassified UK that calling these strikes ‘defensive’ is “a fiction”: the bombers were armed for deep strikes inside Iran.

The martyrs of Minab

On the morning of 28 February, in the middle of the school day, a US missile struck the Shajareh Tayyebeh primary school in Minab, in southern Iran. 168 people were martyred, according to Iranian authorities. Independent researchers have named 157 of them — 123 children aged 13 or younger, 26 school staff and five parents. Months later, the Pentagon has still not published its findings, and the US has never accepted responsibility.

We will not forget the martyrs of Minab. Their names, faces and stories are kept at two memorials: Minab Angels and the Minab School Martyrs Memorial, where survivors share their testimonies.

  • 28 February 2026: the US–Zionist war on Iran begins; Ayatollah Khamenei is assassinated.
  • 28 February 2026: the Minab school massacre — over 168 martyred.
  • 28 February 2026: a US missile hits a sports hall in Lamerd during a women’s volleyball session, killing at least 21, including four children.
  • Over 3,400 killed in Iran by the April ceasefire (UN OCHA), at least 254 of them children (HRANA).
  • From March 2026: US B-1B and B-52 bombers fly against Iran from RAF Fairford, with the British government’s permission (Declassified UK).
  • 8 July 2026: the ceasefire collapses and strikes on Iran resume.

We call forAn end to the war on Iran and the sanctions that isolate Iran from the rest of the globe, justice for the martyrs of Minab and every other martyr killed by the Axis of Evil, and no British part in any aggression against Iran.

05 أذربيجان

Azerbaijan · the right to education

Our sisters in Azerbaijan, kept out of school for wearing the hijab

The Azerbaijani regime, ruling over a population that is 97% Muslim — around 60% of them Shia — has imposed a brutal hijab ban on schoolgirls and teachers. Since 12 October 2026, girls who wear the hijab have been turned away at the school gates, denied the most basic right to an education.

Education is a basic human right, guaranteed to every child under international law. No girl should be forced to choose between her faith and her schooling, and no sister should be expelled from the classroom for choosing to wear the hijab. Denying her an education because of how she dresses is not secularism — it is discrimination.

We will keep protesting for our sisters in Azerbaijan until the ban is lifted and every one of them can return to school.

Key facts

  • 12 October 2026: pupils and teachers wearing the hijab were refused entry to schools across Azerbaijan.
  • 37 people were detained in Baku that day, according to Azerbaijan's Interior Ministry; activists report more than 200 detentions nationwide (Amnesty International).

We call forLift the ban, so that every girl and every teacher can return to school wearing her hijab — and free everyone detained for protesting it.

06 البحرين

Bahrain · persecution of the Shia

Injustices against the Shia of Bahrain

Bahrain's Shia majority has faced more than a decade of systematic repression since the 2011 pro-democracy uprising. Opposition leaders are still serving life sentences, the main Shia opposition society, al-Wefaq, was dissolved, and the senior scholar Sheikh Isa Qassim was stripped of his citizenship.

In 2026 the crackdown deepened. Rights groups report that more than 600 people have been arrested or summoned since February, that over fifty Shia clerics, preachers and reciters have been arrested or prosecuted, and that Ashura was restricted — black clothing banned, banners taken down and mourners, including children, summoned or arrested.

Key facts

  • Prominent opposition leaders have been jailed for more than a decade over the 2011 protests, four of them for life.
  • Ashura 2026: processions restricted and religious symbols removed from public spaces, according to a joint statement by six human rights organisations (3 August 2026).

We call forEnd the persecution of Shias.

07 پاکستان

Pakistan

The massacres of Shias in Pakistan

For decades, Shia Muslims across Pakistan have been hunted for their faith. Mosques and imambargahs have been bombed during Friday prayers, mourners attacked in Muharram, and whole communities targeted — from the Hazara of Quetta to the worshippers of Shikarpur in Sindh. Armed sectarian groups such as Lashkar-e-Jhangvi have openly claimed the killings, while the state has failed to protect those they target.

Nowhere is this clearer than in Kurram district in the north-west, where the mostly Shia city of Parachinar has endured decades of massacres — Pakistan's own Interior Ministry counts 1,405 people killed in twenty years. In November 2024 a convoy of Shia travellers was ambushed near Ochat and more than forty people were killed. The main road was then blockaded for months, cutting off food, fuel and medicine; doctors reported children dying because they could not be treated.

We advocate for every Shia in Pakistan: for their safety, for justice for the victims, and for their right to practise their faith without fear. We also stand with the people of Kashmir, under Indian occupation since the region's special status was revoked in 2019.

Key facts

  • January & February 2013: two bombings targeting the Hazara Shia of Quetta killed more than 100 and at least 91 people; Lashkar-e-Jhangvi claimed both.
  • 30 January 2015: a suicide bombing at the central imambargah in Shikarpur, Sindh, killed more than 50 worshippers at Friday prayers.
  • 21 November 2024: the Ochat convoy attack in Kurram, followed by a months-long blockade of Parachinar.

We call forProtection and justice for the Shia of Pakistan, an end to impunity for those who kill them, and an end to the occupation of Kashmir.

08 البقيع

Saudi Arabia · Medina & the holy places

Jannat al-Baqi and our right to worship in the holy places

Beside the Prophet’s Mosque in Medina lies Jannat al-Baqi, the oldest Islamic cemetery in the city and one of the most sacred places in Islam. Here rest the Prophet’s infant son Ibrahim, his wives, his aunts, Fatima bint Asad — the mother of Imam Ali — and thousands of his companions. Here, too, lie four of the Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt, the Prophet’s own household.

Who are the Shia?

Shia Muslims are those who follow the Ahl al-Bayt. We believe that after the Prophet Muhammad, leadership of the Muslim community passed by divine appointment to Imam Ali ibn Abi Talib, and after him to eleven Imams from his descendants with Fatima al-Zahra, the Prophet’s daughter.

Four of those Imams are buried in al-Baqi: Imam Hasan al-Mujtaba, Imam Ali Zayn al-Abidin, Imam Muhammad al-Baqir and Imam Ja’far al-Sadiq. Many Shia also believe that Fatima al-Zahra herself rests in al-Baqi, in a grave whose place is unknown.

For centuries the graves stood beneath domes and shrines where pilgrims came to pray. In 1806 Wahhabi forces allied with the House of Saud tore them down; the Ottomans rebuilt them. Then, on 8 Shawwal 1344 AH (21 April 1926), the forces of Ibn Saud levelled the cemetery again — the shrines of the Imams, the Prophet’s family and his companions reduced to rubble and bare stones. Every year, on 8 Shawwal, Shia Muslims around the world mourn the day and call for the shrines to be rebuilt. The Saudi authorities have refused every request.

The injustice did not end in 1926. Women are barred from entering al-Baqi and can only look on from behind its fences. Access is tightly limited, and at al-Baqi and the Prophet’s own resting place, Shia pilgrims have long been stopped from reciting supplications, harassed by religious police and even arrested. Shia are prevented from praying in congregation, and Shia communities inside Saudi Arabia have been pressured to stop communal prayers and to close their prayer halls.

Key facts

  • 8 Shawwal 1344 AH (21 April 1926): the shrines of al-Baqi were demolished, after an earlier destruction in 1806.
  • Four Imams of the Ahl al-Bayt, the Prophet’s son Ibrahim, his wives and thousands of companions are buried in al-Baqi.
  • 2009: Saudi security forces cracked down on Shia pilgrims at al-Baqi; dozens were arrested and a 15-year-old was reportedly shot (Human Rights Watch).
  • 2023: men were allowed to recite prayers at al-Baqi during Hajj; women may still only pay respects from behind the fences.

We call forThe shrines of al-Baqi to be rebuilt, the cemetery opened to women and to every pilgrim, and Shia Muslims to be free to pray, in congregation and in their own way, in the holy places.

09 اليمن

Yemen · siege and steadfastness

Break the Saudi siege on Yemen

Since March 2015, a Saudi-led coalition, armed by Britain and the United States, has waged war on Yemen and besieged its people. Its warplanes bombed homes, markets, hospitals, weddings and funerals, while a blockade of Yemen’s ports and airport choked off the food, fuel and medicine the country depends on. A UN-commissioned study estimated that by the end of 2021 the war had killed 377,000 people — most of them through the hunger and disease that the war and the siege created.

In August 2018, a coalition air strike hit a school bus in Dahyan, killing 40 children on a school trip. The bomb was made in the United States. The bombing has largely stopped since the 2022 truce, but the siege has never been lifted, and more than half of Yemen’s people still depend on aid to survive.

And yet Yemen has not bowed. Since October 2023, Yemenis have filled the squares of Sanaa week after week in vast marches for Palestine — some of the largest solidarity demonstrations anywhere in the world. A people under siege, standing up for a people under siege: Yemen inspires us to resist.

Key facts

  • 26 March 2015: the Saudi-led coalition begins its war on Yemen.
  • 9 August 2018: the Dahyan school bus massacre — 40 children killed.
  • 377,000 dead by the end of 2021, according to a UN-commissioned study, most of them from hunger and disease.
  • Since October 2023: weekly mass marches in Sanaa in support of Palestine.

We call forAn end to the siege of Yemen, an end to every British arms sale to Saudi Arabia, and justice for Yemen’s children.

10 هزاره

Afghanistan

Stop the Hazara genocide

The Hazara of Afghanistan, overwhelmingly Shia, have been persecuted and massacred for generations. Today they are hunted where they pray, learn and work: mosques bombed during Friday prayers, schools and study centres attacked, buses and workers targeted. Most of these attacks are claimed by ISIS-K, while the Taliban have done little to protect them — and Taliban fighters have killed Hazara civilians themselves.

In Dasht-e-Barchi, the Hazara neighbourhood of Kabul, a bombing at the Sayed al-Shuhada school in May 2021 killed around 90 people, most of them schoolgirls aged 11 to 15. In September 2022 a suicide bomber struck the Kaaj education centre as students sat a practice university exam, killing 53 — 46 of them girls and young women. We stand with the Hazara and their right to live, pray and learn in safety.

Key facts

  • 8 May 2021: Sayed al-Shuhada school, Kabul — around 90 killed, mostly Hazara schoolgirls.
  • July 2021: Taliban fighters massacre nine Hazara men in Mundarakht, Ghazni province (Amnesty International).
  • 21 April 2022: a bombing at the Seh Dokan Shia mosque in Mazar-i-Sharif kills 31 worshippers.
  • 30 September 2022: Kaaj education centre — 53 killed, 46 of them girls and young women (UN).

We call forAn end to the targeting of the Hazara, protection for their mosques, schools and homes, and justice for every victim.

11 السودان

Sudan

Stop the genocide in Sudan

Since April 2023, Sudan has been torn apart by war between the army and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF), the paramilitary born from the Janjaweed militias that carried out the Darfur genocide two decades ago. Estimates of the dead run from 150,000 to 400,000. Around 15 million people have been driven from their homes — the largest displacement crisis in the world — and famine has been confirmed in Darfur and Kordofan.

In October 2025, after a siege of two and a half years, the RSF stormed El Fasher. The UN documented 6,000 people killed in just three days, and says the true toll is far higher. Fighters asked civilians their tribe before executing them; at the Saudi maternity hospital, around 460 patients, doctors and companions were massacred. A UN fact-finding mission found the hallmarks of genocide against the Fur and Zaghawa peoples.

This genocide is armed from abroad. The United Arab Emirates is accused of supplying the RSF with money, weapons and fighters, and documents seen by the UN Security Council show British-made military equipment in RSF hands, reaching Sudan via the UAE — while Britain kept approving arms exports to the Emirates. Stay updated via @decolonizesudan.

Key facts

  • 15 April 2023: war breaks out between the Sudanese army and the RSF.
  • August 2024: famine confirmed in Zamzam camp, Darfur — later spreading to El Fasher and Kadugli.
  • 26–29 October 2025: the El Fasher massacre — 6,000 killed in three days, documented by the UN.
  • Around 15 million people displaced inside and outside Sudan.

We call forAn end to the genocide in Darfur, an end to every British arms sale to the UAE while it arms the RSF, full access for aid to the starving, and justice for the victims of El Fasher.

12 الهند

India · Hindutva and Islamophobia

Standing against Hindutva and Modi’s Islamophobic policies

India is home to more than 200 million Muslims. Under Narendra Modi and his BJP, Hindutva — the Hindu supremacist ideology of the RSS — has become the policy of the state. Modi was chief minister of Gujarat in 2002, when anti-Muslim pogroms killed more than a thousand people. In 1992, Hindutva mobs tore down the centuries-old Babri Masjid in Ayodhya; in January 2024, Modi himself inaugurated the temple built on its ruins.

Discrimination has been written into law. The Citizenship Amendment Act offers fast-track citizenship to refugees of every major faith in the region — except Muslims. The 2025 Waqf Amendment Act handed the state new powers over mosques, graveyards and endowments held in trust for the Muslim community. Muslim homes, shops and mosques have been bulldozed as collective punishment, so often that India’s own Supreme Court ruled in 2024 that demolishing homes as punishment is illegal — and still the bulldozers come. Muslims are lynched by mobs in the name of ‘cow protection’, while their killers walk free.

We stand with India’s Muslims, and with the people of Kashmir, under occupation since Modi’s government stripped the region of its autonomy in 2019.

Key facts

  • 6 December 1992: the Babri Masjid is demolished by Hindutva mobs.
  • 2002: the Gujarat pogrom — more than 1,000 killed, most of them Muslims, under chief minister Narendra Modi.
  • 5 August 2019: Kashmir’s autonomy revoked and the region placed under lockdown.
  • April 2025: the Waqf Amendment Act becomes law.
  • January–July 2026: 44 Muslims killed in anti-Muslim hate crimes, 19 of them by state forces (India Persecution Tracker).

We call forAn end to Hindutva’s war on India’s Muslims: safety for their lives, homes and mosques, repeal of the CAA and the Waqf Amendment Act, and an end to the occupation of Kashmir.

13 نيجيريا

Nigeria

Free Sheikh Zakzaky — justice for the Shia of Nigeria

Sheikh Ibrahim Zakzaky leads the Islamic Movement in Nigeria, the spiritual home of millions of Shia Muslims. In July 2014 the Nigerian army opened fire on a Quds Day procession in Zaria, killing 35 of his followers — among them three of his sons. In December 2015 the army stormed his home and the movement’s centre in Zaria. A state inquiry counted 348 dead, buried in secret in a mass grave. Three more of his sons were killed, and the Sheikh was seriously wounded and arrested with his wife, Zeenah.

They were held for more than five years, and in 2019 the government banned the Islamic Movement outright. In July 2021 a court acquitted them both of all charges — yet no one has ever been held to account for the Zaria massacre, and the Sheikh’s followers are still attacked when they march: in October 2018 security forces fired on an Arbaeen procession near Abuja, killing dozens by the movement’s count.

Key facts

  • 25 July 2014: 35 killed at a Quds Day procession in Zaria, including three of Sheikh Zakzaky’s sons.
  • 12–14 December 2015: the Zaria massacre — 348 killed according to a state judicial commission, including three more of his sons.
  • July 2019: the Islamic Movement in Nigeria banned.
  • 28 July 2021: Sheikh Zakzaky and his wife acquitted of all charges after more than five years in detention.

We call forJustice for the martyrs of Zaria, an end to the persecution of the Islamic Movement, and full freedom for Sheikh Zakzaky.
