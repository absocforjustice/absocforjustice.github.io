Lift the hijab ban on Azerbaijan’s schoolgirls

Write to Muslim governments and urge them to press Azerbaijan to let our sisters back into school. It takes one minute.

Summary

One email. One minute.

The Azerbaijani regime, ruling over a population that is 97% Muslim, has imposed a brutal hijab ban on schoolgirls and teachers — denying Muslim girls their fundamental right to education.

Iraq now holds the presidency of the Organisation of Islamic Cooperation’s Council of Foreign Ministers. Write to Iraq’s Prime Minister, Ali al-Zaidi, and to the foreign ministers of Muslim-majority countries, and ask them to use their influence to have the ban lifted.

Background

Turned away at the school gates

Since 12 October 2026, Azerbaijan has been enforcing its headscarf ban in schools by refusing entry to every pupil and teacher who wears the hijab. Videos of girls in tears as they are sent home, and of teachers barred from their own classrooms, have spread across the world. It is the same ‘secularism’ excuse Tajikistan used when it outlawed the headscarf.

This is not a ban on a piece of cloth. It is a ban on the Muslim women who wear it — the product of a sustained campaign of Islamophobia that aims to make practising Muslims second-class citizens in their own country.

Azerbaijanis have refused to accept it. Students have walked out, classrooms have emptied as classmates stand with those shut out, and families have protested at the school gates. The regime has answered with a crackdown: its own Interior Ministry says 37 people were detained in Baku on 12 October alone, and activists report more than 200 detentions nationwide. Discrimination on this scale harms everyone in society.

Action required

I

Email Iraq’s Prime Minister

II

Write to the foreign ministers of Muslim-majority countries and demand they take a strong public stand against the ban.
